16. Encryption Technologies

ALL 3 — MILITARY-GRADE STRENGTH

16.1. AES-256-GCM (Primary)

What it is: A bank-vault class standard used by banks, government, and military. 256-bit keys make brute force impractical.

Where we use it: File encryption, on-device data protection, password storage, family profile protection.

16.2. ChaCha20-Poly1305 (Fast)

What it is: A fast, reliable cipher designed for mobile devices. Often faster than AES on phones.

Where we use it: Mobile devices, energy-efficient encryption, in-app data protection.

16.3. XChaCha20-Poly1305 (Maximum)

What it is: An enhanced ChaCha20 variant for high-paranoia scenarios.

Where we use it: Maximum (“Invisible”) mode, especially sensitive data, enterprise users.

All three provide military-grade strength.

Difference is mainly speed and battery impact. Your data stays under layered protection.
